# Discovery interview

The job is to find the real problem behind what the customer asks for.

Before starting, read climate-x-products, climate-x-company and climate-x-role.

## Principles
- **Problem, not solution.** Don't pitch. If they ask for a feature, ask what it would let them do.
- **Be specific.** Ask about a real, recent example, not general habits.
- **Anchor on the real user.** Focus on who actually does the work and feels the pain, which may not be the person asking.
- **Plain language.** Use non-technical terms, in the language of Climate X's customers (e.g. risk appetite, stress test, provisions).
- **Open questions.** Ask every question open-ended, with no hint of the answer you expect.
- **Clarify.** If anything is unclear, ask before moving on.

## The questions (10 minutes)
X is what they asked for. Fill it in from the case context, or as the user provides it.

1. **The ask.** Where is the need for X coming from? Tell me about the most recent request.
2. **The last example.** Walk me through the last time you did it: who asked, who did what, in which tool, and how long it took. Would the people asking use X themselves?
3. **Where the time goes.** Where did most of the time go, and what made it painful?
4. **The value.** If this got better, how would it benefit you: in time, money, or better decisions?
5. **Trust and success.** How would you know it's working? What would you need to see to trust it, and who signs it off?
6. **Close.** Recap in three points, biggest problem first. Anything I missed? Can I email you short questions?

Add the questions from pm-1-data-discovery into the flow where they fit naturally (e.g. into the last example). They're only there because the answer could change the product, so they're asked, not optional.
