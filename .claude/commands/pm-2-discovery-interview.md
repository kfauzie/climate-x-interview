# Discovery interview

The job is to find the real problem behind what the customer asks for.

Before starting, read climate-x-products, climate-x-company and climate-x-role.

## Principles
- **Problem, not solution.** Questions name the problem, never the requested solution. Don't assume who the user is or what gets built. If they ask for a feature, ask what it would let them do.
- **Be specific.** Ask about a real, recent example, not general habits. Name the scenarios (and what each means), the years and the hazards, and say which scenario and year every number uses
- **Plain, specific words.** In the customer's language (e.g. risk appetite, stress test, provisions). Use the customer's own name for their organisation, not a category ("[Company]", not "the company"). Explain any term the reader may not know, in a few words, the first time it appears (a unit, an asset type, a measure). If a plain phrase works, use it instead (for example, say "mortgage application" or "loan application", instead of just "application"). At the same time do not overgeneralize (say "the output", not "the number" if there isn't a number)
- **Open questions.** No hint of the answer you expect and no belief built in.
- **One question per line,** so they get asked one at a time.


## The output: a guide for a curiosity-led call, not a script
X is what they asked for. Fill it in from the case context. No padding: every line must earn its place.

**First, "For prep and review"** (read before the call): the full findings from pm-1-data-discovery, with their numbers, and what any terms mean. Then a divider. Do not re-run pm-1, used the notion generated by pm-1

**Then the guide.** Five sections: Need, User journey, Pain points, Success criteria, Closing. Under each, one or two example questions, framed as prompts, with follow-up prompts in italics between `<>` where they're needed:
- **Need:** Where is the need for X coming from? · Is it just [whoever is asking] asking for this, or do others like you get the same request?
- **User journey:** Walk me through the last time you [did the job behind the ask, in their words]: who did what, in which tool, how long it took. · Who would actually use X? · *\<Unexpected step → "How did you decide that last time?"\>*
  - Name the job, not our product or the data file; they come up in the answer if they matter.
  - If pm-1 found something whose answer could change what gets built, add one line here, stated plainly as an observation: "I noticed [finding in plain words, with a number]." Optionally follow with a short open prompt such as "What's behind that?". Leave it out if nothing is relevant.
- **Pain points:** Where does most of the time go? · Is that the part that hurts most, or is something else worse? · Optionally: What have you tried to fix it? · *\<If something is suspiciously slow → "Why does that happen? It seems simple."\>* · *\<If the customer admits a potential error in their workflow → "What happens when that goes wrong?"\>*
- **Success criteria** (trust sits here): If this worked, how would you know? · What would the impact be: money, time, or better decisions? · What would you need to see to trust the output?
- **Closing:** **\<Recap\>** three points, biggest problem first, in their words and with their numbers. · Anything I missed? · Can I email you short questions?

**Last, an empty Notes section** for the call.
