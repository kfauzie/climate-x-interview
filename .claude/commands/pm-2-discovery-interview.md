# Discovery interview

The job is to find the real problem behind what the customer asks for.

Before starting, read climate-x-products, climate-x-company and climate-x-role.

## Principles
- **Problem, not solution.** Don't pitch. If they ask for a feature, ask what it would let them do.
- **Be specific.** Ask about a real, recent example, not general habits.
- **Anchor on the real user.** Focus on who actually does the work and feels the pain, which may not be the person asking.
- **Plain language.** Use non-technical terms, in the language of Climate X's customers (e.g. risk appetite, stress test, provisions).
- **Open questions.** Ask every question open-ended, with no hint of the answer you expect and no belief built in (not "How do you make sure it's never wrong?").
- **Clarify.** If anything is unclear, ask before moving on.

## The questions (10 minutes)
X is what they asked for. Fill it in from the case context, or as the user provides it.

One question per line: ask it, listen, then ask the next. Never read two out together.

1. **The ask.** Where is the need for X coming from?
2. **The last example.** Walk me through the last time you did it: who asked, who did what, in which tool, and how long it took.
   - Would the people asking use X themselves?
   - *\<If they name a choice or step you didn't expect, ask how it was decided last time\>*
3. **Pain points.** Where did most of the time go?
   - What made it painful?
   - *\<Push when it's vague, hedged or suspiciously slow: "Why does that take two days? It seems simple."\>*
   - *\<If they admit a weakness ("it's not perfectly consistent"), ask "What happens when it isn't?"\>*
4. **The value.** If this got better, how would it benefit you: in time, money, or better decisions?
   - How would you know it's working?
5. **Trust.** What would you need to see to trust it?
   - *\<If they've already covered it, shorten: "You mentioned X. Anything else?"\>*
   - Who signs it off?
6. **Close.** **\<Recap\>** Play it back in three points, biggest problem first, in their words and with their numbers. Anything I missed?
   - Can I email you short questions?

Add the questions from pm-1-data-discovery into the flow where they fit naturally (e.g. into the last example). They're only there because the answer could change the product, so they're asked, not optional.
