# Discovery interview

The job is to find the real problem behind what the customer asks for.

Before starting, read climate-x-products, climate-x-company and climate-x-role.

## Principles
- **Problem, not solution.** Questions name the problem, never the requested solution. Don't assume who the user is or what gets built. If they ask for a feature, ask what it would let them do.
- **Be specific.** Ask about a real, recent example, not general habits.
- **Plain, specific words.** In the customer's language (e.g. risk appetite, stress test, provisions). Use the customer's own name for their organisation, not a category ("[Company]", not "the company"). Say "mortgage application" or "loan application", never just "application". Say "the output", not "the number": there isn't always a number.
- **Open questions.** No hint of the answer you expect and no belief built in.
- **One question per line,** so they get asked one at a time.
- **Stay generic.** Look for patterns, not a known story.

## The output: a guide for a curiosity-led call, not a script
X is what they asked for. Fill it in from the case context. No padding: every line must earn its place.

**At the very top, the checklist:** Need · User journey · Pain points · Success criteria · Closing

**Under each word, one or two example questions, framed as prompts:**
- **Need:** Where is the need for X coming from? · Is it just [whoever is asking] asking for this, or do others like you get the same request?
- **User journey:** Walk me through the last time you [did the job behind the ask, in their words]: who did what, in which tool, how long it took. · Who would actually use X?
  - Name the job, not our product or the data file; they come up in the answer if they matter.
  - If pm-1-data-discovery found something whose answer could change what gets built, add one line here, stated plainly as an observation: "I noticed [finding in plain words, with a number]." Optionally follow with a short open prompt such as "What's behind that?" Never a fixed tag like "Does that match what you see?". Leave it out if nothing is relevant.
- **Pain points:** Where does most of the time go? · What makes it painful?
- **Success criteria** (trust sits here): If this worked, how would you know? · What would you need to see to trust the output?
- **Closing:** **\<Recap\>** three points, biggest problem first, in their words and with their numbers. · Anything I missed?

- Follow-up prompts, in italics between `<>`: *\<Vague, hedged or suspiciously slow → "Why does that happen? It seems simple."\>* · *\<Admits a gap → "What happens when that goes wrong?"\>* · *\<Unexpected step → "How did you decide that last time?"\>* · *\<Before ending → "Can I email you short questions?"\>*
