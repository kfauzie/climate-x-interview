# The role

I'm a Principal PM at Climate X, owning a product area end to end: from a customer's problem to a shipped product that earns revenue. I work in a pod with one engineer, and bring in data and climate scientists as the work needs them. The seat is Customer & UX: discovery-led, with AI fluency expected.

## What good looks like

- **Depth of thinking.** Get past the customer's request to the real workflow and the real problem. Ask the why behind the why.
- **Data-informed.** Back every decision with data and the customer's own words.
- **Simple, valuable solutions.** Find the simplest solution that solves the user's real problem, that users will love, and that makes Climate X more valuable (time saved, consultants avoided, better lending and investment decisions).
- **Prioritise ruthlessly.** Choose the few things that matter most, and explain why: say what you're leaving out.
- **Bias to action.** Move fast and release fast: get a usable version early, then improve it. Make the call on what you have and name the assumption.
- **AI where it earns its place.** Recommend AI where flexibility matters: messy input, free text, interpretation. Recommend code for fixed logic: calculations, rules, checks. If rules are enough, don't recommend a model.
- **A product people trust.** The users are intelligent, non-technical experts making regulated decisions. Show how each answer was produced.
- **Hands-on.** I build it myself, up to a working prototype and a pull request with tests.

## Writing anything the customer sees (questions, screens, documents)

- **Name the thing.** Say exactly what you mean, as they'd see it: name the actual items and rules, not a category word. No vague labels such as "high", "significant" or "at risk": give the measure and the threshold (e.g. "rated [band] or worse", "[n]% of [what]"). Never use words that only make sense after our own work ("the defaults", "the choices", "this", "the method").
- **Explain only what's new to them.** Don't explain what they're expert in (risk, regulation, their own process). Do explain our terms and our assumptions, in a few words, once.
- **Read it as them.** Before showing it: would someone who hasn't seen our notes understand every noun on first reading?

## How to work with me

- Narrate trade-offs: what you're doing, what you're leaving out, and why.
- Challenge me when a choice doesn't trace back to the customer or the data.
