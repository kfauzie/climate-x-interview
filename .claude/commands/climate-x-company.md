# Climate X

From climate-x.com (read November 2026) unless marked (web) for press or third parties. These are their claims, not independently checked. ⚠️ marks where sources disagree.

## Snapshot
- B2B physical climate-risk analytics, based in London. "Google Maps for climate change"
- Founders: Lukky Ahmed (CEO) and Kamil Kluza (COO; one source says CPO ⚠️)
- $18m Series A in June 2024, led by GV (web); about $24.9m raised in total (web). Other investors: WTI, PropTech1, Unconventional Ventures, CommerzVentures, noa
- Advisers include Bob Wigley (Chair of UK Finance) and David Carlin (UNEP FI); science partner CCRM, which started at Oxford
- Claims clients with over $13.5tn in assets, including G-SIBs. ISO certified; works to the PRA's model risk framework
- Products: Spectra, Adapt, Carta, Infrastructure Screener (see `climate-x-products`)

## Mission and values
- Vision: "Make every location on Earth resilient." Mission: "Drive resilient capital, asset by asset"
- Values: win when customers win; tell it like it is; own everything you touch; bet on ourselves and move; have each other's backs

## How the models work
- IPCC approach: hazard × vulnerability × exposure. Damage curves by building use and hazard, calibrated by region
- 11 hazards: river, surface and coastal flood (with or without defences), wildfire, landslide, subsidence, North Atlantic storms, tropical cyclones, storm surge, drought, extreme heat
- Scenarios: RCP 2.6 to 8.5 and SSP1-2.6, 2-4.5, 3-7.0, 5-8.5, in 5-year steps from 2020 to 2100
- Building data on 1.5bn assets (the Spectra page says 2bn ⚠️); documentation and a confidence score per hazard with every report; independent model reviews
- Partnered with Fathom on flood and other perils, October 2025 (web)

## Regulation customers face
- **PRA SS5/25 (UK banks and insurers):** in force since December 2025, replacing SS3/19. Since June 2026 supervisors can ask firms for their gap analysis and evidence. Expects property-level data (not postcode averages), horizons of 2030, 2050 and 2100 under several scenarios, documented data lineage, justified scenario choices including a tail case, and climate risk in capital planning (ICAAP)
- ECB: first penalty on a bank for climate-risk failings, November 2025
- Also relevant: IFRS S2 and TCFD, CSRD, the EU Taxonomy, California SB 261, Australian standards

## Who it sells to
- Banks, insurers, real estate, private equity and asset managers, consultancies, infrastructure
- Partners who resell or build services on the data: CBRE, Aroundtown, IBD Group, Mitie, Scaler, Equans, Embark, Nfq, Triodos (web)

## What it argues publicly
- Bank provisions are too low: models that count only direct damage miss indirect losses 6–14 times larger
- The worst-case scenario SSP5-8.5 is being retired; use SSP3-7.0 plus tail stress tests. The world is currently tracking close to SSP2-4.5
- Wildfire (with CBRE): smoke, power cuts and insurance withdrawal reach far more assets than the fires themselves
- Engineering: real-time queries over 240TB of hazard data at 30m resolution

## Competitors (web)
- Moody's RMS (ranked first by Chartis in 2025; sold alongside credit data), Jupiter (sells on model transparency), S&P (bought The Climate Service), XDI, EarthScan, Mitiga
- Climate X's answer: its own models across all hazards, full documentation, financial loss rather than hazard scores, self-serve speed

## Brand and product look
- Font: Graphik (Helvetica, Arial as fallbacks). Colours: red `#e01e1a` (darker `#a11b14` on hover), charcoal `#343a40`, off-white `#f9f9fa`, white panels
- Product screens: light, white panels, thin dividers, small grey uppercase labels; scenario and year as rounded dropdowns in a side panel; red for active toggles; A–G ratings as coloured circles; large light-weight numbers; pill buttons; red only for the primary action

## My take
- For UK banks, SS5/25 is the "why now": every number has to be defensible, with its assumptions and lineage
- Their own story (full documentation, financial loss, every number traceable) sets the bar for anything I build
