# Adapt and Adaptation Finance

## What it is
- Adaptation cost and ROI per asset, report in ~60 seconds (CSV): pre-adaptation risk, implementation cost, 1- and 10-year ROI, loss savings, post-adaptation risk
- E.g. SSP2-4.5, flood defences on: building loss £33,828 → £5,674; river flood E → A
- Built on ISO 14090/14091, UNFCCC, EU Taxonomy
- Uses: due diligence, valuation, undervalued assets, financing, insurance negotiation. Replaces weeks of consultants

## Adaptation Finance (for banks)
- "$2tn a year" resilience funding gap
- Bank names corporate clients → Carta maps assets → highest-ROI projects → risk engineers do due diligence → bank finances it
- E.g. Hilton: 143 assets, $26.5m financing, ~$0.8m fees over 5 years. Hindalco: 8,145 assets, $680m

## Who buys it
- Real-estate owners (Aroundtown, CBRE), facilities partners (Mitie, Equans, IBD), banks (Triodos for SME lending; corporate banking for Finance)

## Gaps (my take)
- Cost and effectiveness assumptions vary by site; need to be visible and editable
- ROI depends on horizon, discount rate, scenario: same judgment-call problem as Spectra
- Avoided loss is hard to prove after the fact
- Different buyer (capex, relationship managers) → a new champion each time

## Opportunities (my take)
1. Resilience-linked loans: completed adaptation lowers the rating and the price
2. Capex optimiser: most avoided loss per £ within a budget
3. Editable assumptions per measure, with source and confidence
4. Relationship-manager flow: Adapt result → financing proposal
5. Track realised value against later events

## Questions
- After you see a high-risk asset, what happens next? Who decides to spend?
- Last adaptation or retrofit decision: what did the business case look like?
- (Banks) When did resilience last come up with a corporate client? What happened next?
