# Run 1: discovery call with Sarah Okafor (role-play), notes

Source: Notion meeting notes, https://app.notion.com/p/40b8f31ef05f80f38b1dc745fa3f70fe (inside the run 1 questions page, https://app.notion.com/p/40b8f31ef05f8114908bd8ada551932c). Fetched 2026-11-01.

## Key statements (verbatim where quoted)
- **Volume:** "Questions come in every week from all over the bank, about 15 a month"
- **Recent questions:**
  - CRO: "total expected climate loss by 2050", then "which hazard and region were driving it" (another 2 days)
  - Credit committee: "how many commercial real estate loans have a flood rating of E or worse by 2050"
  - Treasury: "how much expected losses rise from 2030 to 2050 under the severe scenario"
- **Repeats:** "Mostly one-offs… The only one that repeats is the quarterly risk committee pack, which is exposure by hazard, region, and scenario"
- **Who asks:** CRO, credit committee, treasury, mortgage pricing, investor relations, "by email, on Teams, or in meetings, or senior people asking in plain English"
- **Workflow (CRE flood example):** email → analyst pulls latest Spectra export into Excel → filtering "maybe an hour" → "the rest went on deciding which scenario and horizon to use, whether to include loans in arrears, and then writing up how we got the number so it stands up" → Sarah checks → "All in about 3 days"
- **Hedge:** asked whether most time went on deciding scenarios: "Roughly, yes, which is exactly why I want my analysts configuring reports in Spectra directly rather than wrestling with a spreadsheet of hundreds of columns"
- **Most painful:** "Honestly, it's the checking. Every answer comes through me before it leaves the team, because one subtly wrong scenario choice and the number means something different. So things queue up on my desk"
- **What she checks:** "the scenario and horizon match what was actually asked, that the right loans are in or out, and that the totals reconcile back to the book. Then I read the write-up to make sure someone senior could follow it. That alone can take me half a day when it's a big one and I'm doing several a week"
- **Other data:** "the loan data from our own systems, balances, arrears, status, property type… matching on loan ID. It usually lines up, but when it doesn't, that's another few hours gone"
- **Size:** "Each one takes 2 to 5 days end to end, so that's roughly 45 analyst days a month, plus a good chunk of my own time on the checking"
- **Trust:** "model risk would have to approve it, and they'll only sign off if every number is computed by code and traceable back to the data. If it's some AI making up figures, it's dead on arrival. I'd need to see exactly how each figure was produced, the same way my analysts write it up today"
- **Sign-off:** model risk, IT, procurement; "My CRO would have to back it too, so it has to be worth the hassle"
- **Correction at recap:** "The bit you're underplaying is that it all runs through me, so I'm the bottleneck, and the answers rarely end there because someone always comes back with, what about the severe scenario? And we start again"
- **Next steps:** follow-up with "something concrete though, not slides"; she'll bring an analyst, and "if it involves AI, someone from model risk should hear it early"; email OK, "copy my analysts if it's about the data"; she'll send example questions and what they produced this week, "nothing with real loan data on it until procurement has signed something"
